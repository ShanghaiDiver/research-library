# Rolls-Royce: engine flying hours, operating economics and the November decision gate

**Research date:** 9 November 2026. **Status:** supplementary monitoring note for the existing RR thesis; **not** a new independently audited fair value or trade instruction.

## Executive finding

Independent fleet activity suggests **Rolls-Royce's own installed fleet flying hours continue to rise** despite weak global passenger demand: BNP Paribas Exane's Cirium-based tracker recorded +3.0% year on year in October and +3.4% for July–October. That tends to *contradict a thesis of presently collapsing RR engine flying hours*, but does **not** alone prove that free cash flow will meet the company's targets. Shop-visit costs, LTSA contract economics and durability remain decisive.

The next direct verification is Rolls-Royce's **ten-month trading update scheduled for 12 November 2026**. Do not portray it as reported before that date.

**Measurement cautions:** IATA RPK measures paid passenger kilometres (passenger load × distance). RR EFH measures time flown by engines covered by the relevant contract arrangements. Cirium's fleet tracker is estimated aircraft activity for RR-powered aircraft, not RR's audited contractual invoicing data. Quarterly flow and annual index levels should not be conflated; don't add quarterly growth percentages. BNP reports its tracker is 99.4% correlated with RR reported flying hours historically; this is a broker claim, not independently reproduced here. RR is predominantly exposed to **widebody**, whereas IATA's total includes short-haul and domestic traffic.

## The cash bridge: where the real risk resides

- H1 2026 **large engine major shop visits: 294 versus 217 in H1 2025 (+35%)**, while total LTSA shop visits (large + business aviation/regional) were **712 versus 696 (+2%)**. Differences in mix matter, and total visits are not comparable to large major visits alone.
- H1 2026 LTSA invoiced flying-hour receipts: **£3.4bn versus £3.0bn** in H1 2025, reflecting growth in hours and improved contracted rates.
- Civil Aerospace net LTSA balance growth net of risk/revenue-sharing: **£86m vs £472m** in H1 2025. More shop work consumes cash and supply-chain cost pressures persist.
- H1 Civil Aerospace service revenue increased **26%**, and LTSA revenue catch-ups were **£356m vs £126m**, with part of profit improvement arising from higher contract profitability and catch-up accounting. **Do not extrapolate these one-offs** as recurring organic growth.
- RR is improving engine *time on wing*. That can reduce maintenance events and economic service costs for a given level of flying hours, but it needs to be assessed against the timing/cash costs of nearer-term shop visits and contractual obligations.

**Monitoring chain:** engine-specific utilisation → billed EFH and rates → LTSA cash receipts → major shop visit volume/cost/time on wing → net LTSA balance → group free cash flow. Track *all* links, not EFH alone.

## Valuation snapshot, not a new house value

- **6 November 2026 official market close (external price service): 1,261.2p**. 9 November is trading around **1,290p intraday**; do not call this a final close.
- Equity market capitalisation around **£106bn** at ~1,290p (indicative).
- Company upgraded FY2026 FCF guidance in July to **£3.8–4.0bn**, midpoint **£3.9bn**; headline **FCF yield ≈3.7%** on ~£106bn equity value (not a fully normalised distributable owner yield).
- September 2026 sell-side consensus on RR site: **2026 EPS 43.7p** and **FCF £4.086bn**, giving approximately **29.5×** consensus FY26 EPS at 1,290p and **~3.9%** FY26 consensus FCF/equity value.
- February 2026 management **2028 FCF target £5.0–5.3bn**, midpoint **£5.15bn**, implying **~4.9%** of current equity value if achieved. Targets are NOT guaranteed and don't include a separate discounting of future cash, LTSA balances or other cash claims.
- The old June **1,310p** house value was explicitly declared historical in the October report. The current price near that number does **not** automatically create a value signal; earnings and risks have changed.

These simple yields are perspective only. A complete prospective-return assessment needs 2028–2032 FCF per share after buybacks and dilution, dividend/payout, capital reinvestment, net cash/debt, required shop investment, cash tax and a justifiable terminal multiple. Buybacks reduce the denominator over time but consume cash; **do not add gross buybacks again as independent shareholder returns** when modelling per-share value.

## Election/geopolitical context

The November 2026 US Democratic congressional wins could change the policy backdrop for defence spending, government programmes, trade, and aerospace demand, but the direction for RR's Defence orders is **not established by the election alone**. The nearer-term direct civil aviation risks include Middle East travel disruption, jet-fuel prices and airlines' ability to sustain widebody utilisation. Separate macro/political drivers from observed RR engine-hour data.

## Decision gate on 12 November 2026

**Green / supports staged consideration:** RR-specific engine hours remain positive and broadly consistent with Cirium tracking; no material cut to £3.8–4.0bn FY26 FCF guidance; no deterioration in large-engine shop capacity or LTSA unit costs; 2028 cash objectives remain credible.

**Amber / investigate before purchase:** engine hours positive but invoiced receipts, net LTSA balance and cash conversion weaken due to costly visits, or guidance depends heavily on catch-ups.

**Red / downgrade thesis:** materially weaker RR engine hours, guidance cut, persistent shop bottlenecks/durability problems or contract economics deteriorating; don't mistake lower share price for an investment thesis.

**Portfolio:** A small existing RR position may justify an increase on superior expected returns, not simply to achieve a £4–5k round-number holding. Position changes are a decision across SIPP + ISA + taxable account. Keep the appreciated index-fund hedge and the liquidity-replenishment plan in mind, but do not force a sale to create a purchase.
