Watch. A credible recovery in operating profits and debt, but not yet proof of resilient per-share owner cash flow.

The business and franchise

PZ Cussons owns Carex, Imperial Leather, Original Source, Sanctuary Spa, Cussons Baby, Morning Fresh and St.Tropez. These are established brands but not unassailable global franchises: local price competition, retailers, advertising costs and Nigeria's currency regime constrain the durability of returns. The strongest case is for a focused everyday-consumer-goods portfolio with genuine routes to market; the weaker case is overpaying for branded acquisitions and mistaking nominal growth in Nigeria for sterling shareholder returns.

FY2026 operating evidence

Revenue rose to £541.4m from £513.8m (+5.4%); like-for-like growth was 5.8%. Adjusted operating profit reached £59.5m from £54.9m (+8.4%). Excluding the previous year's now-sold Wilmar joint venture, growth was 24.5%, a substantially different comparison. Adjusted margin was 11.0% versus 10.7%. Yet adjusted EPS declined to 7.14p from 7.34p. Underlying improvement is real, but it has not yet translated into EPS compounding.

Earnings quality and the balance sheet

Net debt was reduced to about £25m from £112m. Disposals, notably the Wilmar stake, helped the reduction, so the £87m change is not an annual recurring cash-flow rate. Nigeria is another reason for caution: favourable currency developments helped FY26, but local cash generation, remittance, imported inputs and devaluation can create a mismatch between brand sales and shareholder cash. St.Tropez returned to growth in North America after distribution changes, but an emerging recovery in tanning products is not a secure long-term annuity.

Capital allocation and management

Jonathan Myers has simplified the portfolio and reset investment priorities. The resumption of modest dividend growth is encouraging; it is not sufficient evidence of durable owner earnings. Test growth in free cash flow after brand investment and sustaining capex, return on invested capital after acquisitions, and whether a less leveraged balance sheet can withstand another Nigeria shock. There is also an opportunity cost: compared with P&G and other defensive holdings, PZC carries much higher business and geographical execution risk.

Valuation and ten-year ownership test

The 26 October share close of 95.60p is close to the old index 94p fair-value reference. The historical 75p buy threshold and 120p valuation-review threshold cannot be justified without the missing valuation model. The price is about 13.4× FY26 adjusted EPS, but cheap-looking multiples on weak free cash can be a trap. The indicated historical dividend yield is ~3.9%; payout and growth require assessment against cash, not merely earnings. A proper fresh fair value should use independently modelled recurring post-disposal earnings, Nigeria FX and cash repatriation, brand investment, and discounted cash flow with explicit bear/base/bull cases.

Bear: currency shock and retailer competition weaken per-share cash. Base: modest organic growth, stable margins and lower debt. Bull: broad brand growth and sustained St.Tropez economics produce superior cash returns. Without modelled owner FCF, none earns a defensible precise price today.

Investment decision and falsification

WATCH — not a blanket 'value trap' dismissal, but still not a proven buy.

Upgrade if EPS and recurring FCF rise on the same post-disposal perimeter for multiple periods, Nigeria cash conversion holds and St.Tropez contributes profitably. Avoid if operating improvement depends on currency translation, disposal gains or repeated adjustments while underlying cash returns stagnate. No automatic price-based trimming: eventual exposure decisions depend on the actual portfolio.
