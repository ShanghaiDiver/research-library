01

The business, and why you are watching it

Antofagasta mines copper in Chile, principally at Los Pelambres and Centinela, with gold and molybdenum as valuable by-products. It sits towards the top end of pure-play copper producers on margin and cost, controlled by the Luksic family, who own ~65% and run it for the long term. You have it on the watchlist for good reason: it is a higher-quality, purer copper play than the Glencore you already own, and copper is the one commodity with a clean structural-demand story, grids, renewables, EVs and now data-centre power all need far more of it, against a supply side of ageing mines, falling grades and slow permitting.

02

The quality is real, and the growth is funded

2025 was a record year: revenue up 30% to $8.6bn, EBITDA up 52% to $5.2bn at a 60% margin, underlying earnings up 106%, all on a realised copper price of $4.93/lb. Crucially the balance sheet stayed pristine, net debt of just 0.53 times EBITDA (0.68x by mid-2026) despite $3.7bn of investment, and net cash costs fell to multi-year lows (~$1.19/lb). The growth is the differentiator: the Centinela second concentrator and a Los Pelambres uplift are funded, on time and on budget, completing in 2027 and ramping to deliver ~30% more copper by 2029. Few miners offer volume growth this large at costs this low.

So on the quality floor, Antofagasta passes with room: high returns (cyclically very high now), a fortress balance sheet, real cash generation, a resource moat you cannot replicate, and a growth pipeline. The debate is not the business, it is the copper price and where in the cycle you are buying.

03

The catch: near highs, on record copper

The shares have re-rated from a ~1,300p low to near 1,950p as copper strengthened, so 2025's record earnings are struck on an elevated price. That cuts both ways. If copper stays high or climbs on the structural deficit, today's earnings hold and the 30% volume growth compounds on top, and the shares are reasonable. If copper mean-reverts in a global slowdown, earnings fall hard, mining is operationally geared, and a stock bought near its high takes the full drawdown. Buying a cyclical on peak-ish prices is the classic way to be right about the company and still lose money for a while.

04

The case, both ways

Bull. The best kind of miner, low-cost, long-life, fortress balance sheet, family-aligned, growing volumes 30% at the bottom of the cost curve, plugged into the one commodity with a decade-long structural deficit. If the copper bulls are right, this is not a cyclical at all but a structural grower, and 1,950p will look cheap.

Bear. It is still a price-taker on a volatile commodity, the earnings are struck on a high copper price, the shares are near their peak, and China, the swing buyer of copper, is uncertain. Growth projects run late and over budget across the industry as a rule, and a copper downcycle would take the shares down 30%-plus regardless of how good the mine is.

Variant perception

The market is pricing Antofagasta as a good cyclical near the top of its cycle, hence the shares tracking copper up to the highs. The variant view worth holding is that copper is not really cyclical from here but structurally short: electrification and grid build-out raise demand for years while supply cannot respond quickly, so the "peak" price may be a floor. If that is right, Antofagasta is a rare miner that is also a grower, and you are not buying the top. The honest counter is that this is a macro bet on copper dressed as a stock pick, and the framework's discipline on cyclicals is explicit, buy them when the commodity is weak and the shares are hated, not when both are strong. So the verdict is not that Antofagasta is dear, it is fair, but that the risk/reward is far better after a pullback. Watch it, and want it lower, or hold it small as a deliberate structural-copper position rather than a value buy.

05

Currency and fit, for a sterling holder

This one earns a note because it does something your book badly needs. Copper is priced in dollars and the mines are in Chile, so economically Antofagasta is a dollar-revenue, Latin-American real-asset business in a sterling quote, genuinely diversifying against a portfolio that is ~70% US and heavily technology. It is a real-asset, electrification play whose cycle has little to do with the S&P. The caveat is that you already hold Glencore for diversified mining; Antofagasta would be the higher-quality, pure-copper complement or replacement, not additive resource risk on top. As a structural-copper and diversification holding it has a real role; the only question the framework insists on is paying the right price for the cycle.

06

What would change the call

  • Upgrade to Buy on a copper-driven pullback toward ~1,600p (a real margin of safety on a cyclical), or as a small deliberate starter now if you specifically want structural-copper and diversification exposure and can sit through the volatility.
  • Downgrade if the growth projects slip badly or over-run on cost, if Chilean tax or permitting risk rises materially, or if copper's structural-deficit thesis breaks (a genuine demand shock).
07

Verdict

A genuinely high-quality copper grower with a real structural tailwind, priced fairly but bought near the top of the copper cycle. Track B. Fair value ~1,965p against ~1,950p. Action: Watch, buy below ~1,600p or as a small deliberate structural-copper starter.

Your instinct to watch Antofagasta is sound: it is the best-in-class way to own copper, the one commodity with a clean multi-decade demand story, from a low-cost operator with a fortress balance sheet and funded 30% volume growth. What the framework insists on is respecting the cyclicality. Buying a price-taker near its 52-week high, on record realised prices, is exactly the entry the value track is designed to avoid, however good the mine. So this is a Watch that wants to be a Buy at a better price, and the trigger is copper weakness rather than any change in the company. The one nuance that tilts in its favour for your book specifically is fit: as a dollar-earning, Latin-American, real-asset, electrification play, it is a genuine diversifier against a very US-and-tech-heavy portfolio, which is a reason to want it, though not a reason to overpay for the cycle.
