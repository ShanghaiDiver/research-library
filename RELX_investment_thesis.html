RELX: AI disruption is a hypothesis, not yet a financial result

Watch for new investment at 2,992p (26 October close); own for the long term if already held and the thesis remains intact. We previously called Buy at 2,627p, with a 3,075p intrinsic estimate and a 2,900p entry threshold. At 2,992p, that prior value offers only 2.8% headline valuation headroom, although long-term compounding can justify ownership above a static value estimate. Do not chase the recovery merely to correct a missed entry.

The evidence, division by division

The 22 October nine-month update showed 7% underlying group revenue growth, with Risk +8%, Legal +10%, STM +6% and Exhibitions +4%. Legal's acceleration is particularly inconsistent with an immediate-collapse AI narrative. But current growth alone cannot reveal whether a new generation of AI agents will weaken future renewal pricing or disintermediate customer workflows.

Where the moat actually lies

Generating an answer is not the same as supplying authoritative data and defensible decisions. Legal content provenance, case histories, verification and the ability to cite binding authorities may remain scarce even if language models become cheap. Risk data networks are an arguably stronger moat: fraud and identity decisions depend on data permissions, identity resolution, contributed events and loss histories, not just model intelligence. STM has a more nuanced exposure: AI may improve discovery and analytics while threatening interfaces and lower-value publishing services. Thus AI could expand usage and value in one division while commoditising another.

Financial quality and capital allocation

H1 2026 underlying sales rose 7%; adjusted operating-profit growth was 9%, adjusted operating margin increased to 35.5%, cash conversion was approximately 98%, and net debt/EBITDA was 2.3×. These reinforce quality, not immunity. Substantial buybacks alongside higher debt deserve separate evaluation: if a company borrows to repurchase shares, higher EPS need not mean higher intrinsic value. We should track the actual incremental return on data acquisitions, technology spend and buybacks, plus whether recurring subscriptions have retained pricing power.

For future disruption scares, staged initial entries may be appropriate when downside economics are bounded and balance-sheet strength is demonstrated; that is a research principle, not permission to buy every falling share.

What would resolve the thesis?

Support: renewal rates and pricing remain robust, AI products expand billings without materially raising serving costs, Legal and Risk retain the authoritative data relationship, and free cash flow per share compounds. Refute: lost client workflows, accelerating price concessions, content-cost inflation, customer replacement of high-value tools with AI agent alternatives, or materially weaker cash conversion. Revisit management disclosures and competitor developments at each results cycle; analyst price targets indicate sentiment, not value. An existing holder's eventual trim decision will depend on portfolio concentration as well as changes to the thesis.

01

The business

RELX sells information-based analytics and decision tools to professionals who cannot do their jobs without them. Four segments: Risk (data and analytics for insurers, banks and government, its highest-margin engine), Scientific, Technical & Medical (research journals and databases, including the ScienceDirect and Scopus franchises), Legal (LexisNexis), and Exhibitions (trade shows). Roughly two-thirds of revenue is recurring subscription, the assets are largely intangible, and capital intensity is low, which is why a business this size earns 15%-plus on capital. It is a London listing but an overwhelmingly overseas earner, more than half in the US, with the rest across Europe and Asia.

02

Why it screens, and why it is cheap

RELX clears the quality floor more emphatically than almost anything in the index: return on invested capital of 15.4%, and rising for five straight years; net debt of 2.0 times EBITDA; cash conversion of 99%; a share count falling under a £2.25bn buyback; and underlying revenue growth of a metronomic 7% with adjusted EPS up ~10%. Yet the shares have re-rated down from a historic low-30s multiple to about 20 times, and traded as low as ~2,030p over the past year. The reason is a single fear: that generative AI will disintermediate its data and legal franchises, the way open-access once threatened its journals.

That is the setup the framework is built to exploit: a genuine compounder marked down on a fear, rather than on any deterioration in the numbers, which have if anything improved.

03

The long-term return test

On the v2 method, the question is not whether 20 times is cheap but what a decade-long owner earns from here after a stress. Underlying EPS compounds at roughly 8-9% (7% revenue, a little margin, and the buyback shrinking the count), the dividend yields 2.6%, and total shareholder yield is ~6%. So before any multiple change, the owner earns ~11% a year. Hold the multiple at 20 times and that is the return; a modest re-rating adds to it, a de-rating subtracts. The scenarios, in pence:

The base case clears the 10% hurdle; the bear, which needs both a growth halving and a further de-rating, is the honest cost of the AI uncertainty. No analyst target here, by design, the return does the talking.

04

The case, both ways

Bull. A rare compounder, 15%-plus returns, 7% growth, 99% cash conversion, a fortress of proprietary data and workflow lock-in, handing back 6% a year, at 20 times after an unjustified de-rating. AI needs exactly the trusted, licensed, structured data RELX owns, and RELX is already embedding it into higher-value tools.

Bear. Twenty times is not cheap in absolute terms, and if large language models can answer legal and scientific questions directly, the subscription moats that justify the multiple erode. Open access has chipped at STM for years; GenAI could do to Legal what it threatens to do to search.

Variant perception

The market is pricing RELX as an AI victim, a data toll-booth about to be bypassed. The variant view is that it is an AI beneficiary: the value in law and science is not the raw answer but the verified, citable, liability-bearing, permissioned source behind it, and that is precisely what a hallucination-prone model cannot supply and must license. RELX owns the corpora, the customer relationships and the workflow, and is shipping AI features that raise the value of its subscriptions rather than cannibalise them. The bear's fair point is that this is a thesis, not yet a proof, and the multiple, though de-rated, still assumes durability. The honest read: the balance of evidence favours the beneficiary case, the numbers keep improving through the AI scare, and you are being asked to pay a fair-not-cheap price for a genuinely exceptional business. That is a Buy on the compounder track, with the AI question as the one thing to watch like a hawk.

05

Currency, for a sterling holder

Worth stating plainly given your book: RELX is quoted in pence but earns most of its money in dollars, with the rest in euros and other currencies. So although it fills the "quality UK compounder" slot, economically it deepens the US exposure the look-through already put near 70%. It is a better way to own US earnings than another S&P tracker, a specific high-return franchise rather than the index, but it is not diversification away from America. Size it in that knowledge.

06

What would change the call

  • Downgrade if underlying growth slips below ~5%, if STM or Legal show real AI-driven attrition (falling renewals, pricing pressure), or if the multiple runs back above ~28× without a growth acceleration to justify it.
  • Conviction rises if RELX's AI products show up as accelerating organic growth or pricing, which would convert the variant thesis into fact and support a re-rating.
07

Verdict

A genuinely exceptional compounder, marked down on an AI fear the evidence does not yet support. Track A. Fair value ~3,075p against ~2,627p, base return ~11% a year. Action: Buy, add around here, sized as a core holding.

RELX is the clearest vindication of the screen and of v2 together: a business that clears every quality gate with room to spare, throws off cash, and has been handed to patient buyers at a reasonable multiple because the market is worried about a disruption that, on the evidence, is more likely to feed it than starve it. The base-case return comfortably clears the hurdle and the quality is not in doubt; the risk is concentrated in one question, AI, which the note names as the thing to monitor and the trigger to reconsider. It becomes the book's second Buy after Microsoft, and like Microsoft it is a case of buying quality when a fear makes it fair, not when momentum makes it dear. The one caveat that is yours to weigh is currency: this is US earnings in a sterling wrapper, so it adds to a US tilt that is already high.
