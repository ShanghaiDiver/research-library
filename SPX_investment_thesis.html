Spirax: recovery in profits must become recovery in cash

Watch / selectively accumulate only after validating cash recovery. The 26 October London close was 7,360p. Our August house value of 7,800p implies around 6% value headroom on the historic assumptions, while the old 6,500p buy threshold remains a conservative entry reference. This is not a newly completed discounted owner-cash valuation.

H1 2026 results — underlying versus accounting recovery

Spirax generated £863.8m sales, up 5% reported and organically, and £171.1m adjusted operating profit, up 8% reported and 6% organic. The adjusted operating margin was 19.8%, 50bp higher reported and 10bp better organically. Adjusted EPS rose 9% to 150.0p. Reported operating profit jumped 44% to £154.2m, but the comparison is distorted by restructuring costs in 2025. Conversely adjusted cash conversion was only 54%, down from 61%, reflecting seasonality and planned inventory builds according to management. That is the most important qualification to an apparently improving result.

Three divisions, three readings

Steam Thermal Solutions organic revenue grew just 1%, despite demand growth above industrial production and a healthy order book; mix, shipment timing and growth investments depressed the margin. Electric Thermal Solutions grew 11%, benefiting from activity in all three divisions; Watson-Marlow Fluid Technology Solutions grew 7%, with biopharma orders ahead of sales. This mix provides some resilience but does not justify extrapolating one strong product cycle through every end market. The secular investment case rests on process efficiency, electrification, precision pumping and demanding customer specification rather than a generic industrial rebound.

Management, reinvestment and the premium

Management maintained full-year expectations of mid-single-digit organic growth with organic margin progression. A high recurring aftermarket/service content, customer process dependence and technical leadership support premium economics. However, the difference between strong returns on tangible capital and more moderate returns including acquisition goodwill requires scrutiny. Confirm that prior acquisitions and growth investment produce cash returns above the cost of capital. Sustained low cash conversion, capitalised costs or working-capital build would undercut the long-term compounder thesis even if adjusted margin recovers.

Illustratively, 8% annual EPS growth with 20% multiple compression over 10 years produces around 5.6% annual share-price growth before dividends and tax; 11% with an unchanged multiple produces 11%. Alternative investments and what would falsify the thesis

Against RELX, Spirax has more industrial cyclicality, tangible product and inventory exposure, and a slower organic growth engine today. Against GAW, it has more diversified end markets but substantially more manufacturing and acquisition-integration exposure. The moat is plausible; the premium should be earned through sustainable cash returns. Watch STS backlog conversion, WMFTS biopharma order quality, inventory release in H2, adjusted-to-statutory differences and ROIC including goodwill. Upgrade with sustained mid-single-digit organic growth and full-year cash normalisation; downgrade if working-capital demands persist or cyclicality reveals a structurally weaker aftermarket. Actual portfolio concentration is a later, independent decision.

01

The business

Spirax does dull things that matter enormously. Its Steam Thermal and Electric Thermal businesses keep industrial processes at the right temperature, and Watson-Marlow makes the peristaltic pumps that move fluids through pharmaceutical and biotech manufacturing without contamination. Around 85% of sales come from recurring maintenance and small improvements to equipment already installed in customers' plants, which is why it earns 35% on capital and has raised its dividend for over 55 years. It is genuinely global, EMEA 44%, Americas 38%, Asia-Pacific 18%, and mission-critical: when a customer's plant depends on your kit, they do not switch to save a few pounds. That is a real moat.

02

Why it screens, and why it crashed

On the quality floor Spirax is exceptional: return on capital employed of 35%, a fortress-ish balance sheet, high recurring revenue, no dilution, a durable moat. But the shares have collapsed, from a peak near 16,000p to about 7,300p, and roughly 40% in the past year alone, because two of its end-markets went into a synchronised downturn: a brutal destock in biopharma hit Watson-Marlow after the pandemic surge, and general industrial and semiconductor weakness, especially in China, hit the thermal businesses. Organic growth, once high-single to double-digit, slowed to about 5%. The premium multiple the market once paid, 35-40 times, unwound to about 20.

One honest flag the screen surfaces: ROCE of 35% describes the core operating business, but return on invested capital, which counts the goodwill paid for acquisitions like Chromalox and Vulcanic, is only ~13%. Still above the cost of capital, but a reminder that Spirax's acquisitions have not earned at the rate its own operations do. The underlying business is elite; the capital allocation has been merely good.

03

RELX or Diageo? The long-term return test

The useful way to judge a crashed compounder is to ask which kind it is. RELX fell on a fear its numbers contradicted, so it was a Buy. Diageo fell on troubles its numbers confirmed, with leverage and a dividend cut, so it was a Watch. Spirax sits between them: unlike RELX, its numbers really did soften, growth stalled and earnings were downgraded; but unlike Diageo, there is no balance-sheet distress, no dividend cut, and the cause is a finite cyclical destock rather than an open structural question. On the return test, if organic growth recovers toward 7-8% as the destock ends, the 2.3% yield and a hold of the ~20x multiple deliver ~10% a year, with a re-rating on top; if growth stays stuck at 5%, the return is nearer 7%. 04

The case, both ways

Bull. A rare, genuinely elite compounder, 35% returns, 85% recurring revenue, a real moat, 55 years of dividend growth, at 20 times after a brutal but cyclical de-rating. The biopharma destock is finite and the pumps come back; industrial recovers; and you rarely get this quality this cheap. Buy great businesses when their end-markets are in the doldrums.

Bear. The recovery is a forecast, not a fact, and management has been optimistic before, earnings estimates have been cut repeatedly, so the "turn" keeps being pushed out. Twenty times still isn't cheap for a business growing 5%, biopharma may reset lower rather than rebound, and Chinese industrial demand is uncertain. A de-rated compounder can keep de-rating if the growth doesn't come.

Variant perception

The market is pricing Spirax as a broken compounder whose growth has permanently downshifted, hence a multiple less than half its old level. The variant view is that the moat, the returns and the recurring revenue are all intact, and only the cycle has turned, so this is a temporary trough in a structurally excellent business, and the de-rating has handed patient buyers a quality name at a fair price for the first time in a decade. The honest tension is that, unlike RELX, the numbers give the bears something real to point at, the growth did stall, the estimates did fall, and the turn is genuinely unproven, not merely feared. So the framework's answer is neither the bull's "obvious buy" nor the bear's "avoid": it is a Watch that is close. The quality clears the floor comfortably and the price is finally reasonable; what is missing is evidence that organic growth is re-accelerating rather than merely being promised. Buy it below ~6,500p, where the margin of safety pays for the uncertainty, or on the first clear sign the destock has ended.

05

Currency and fit, for a sterling holder

A point in its favour for your book. Spirax earns across EMEA (44%), the Americas (38%) and Asia-Pacific (18%), so it is genuinely globally diversified rather than another slug of US exposure, and it brings real European and Asian economic weight, some of the ex-US international you are short of, inside a sterling-listed, easy-to-track wrapper. Unlike RELX, which deepens the US tilt, Spirax helps the geography gap. The dollar and euro translation cuts both ways year to year, but as a diversifier it does a job the trackers do not.

06

What would change the call

  • Upgrade to Buy on the first hard evidence the turn is real, Watson-Marlow order intake recovering, organic growth re-accelerating above ~6%, estimates stabilising or rising, or simply on a lower price toward ~6,500p.
  • Downgrade towards Avoid if organic growth stays stuck below 5% for another year, if biopharma resets structurally lower rather than recovering, or if further acquisitions dilute returns.
07

Verdict

An elite compounder at a fair price for the first time in years, but the growth stalled and the turn is unproven. Track A. Fair value ~7,800p against ~7,300p, base return ~10%. Action: Watch, close to a Buy, add below ~6,500p or on clear evidence of recovery.

Spirax is the case that directly answers the nervousness a name like this provokes. It fell hard, which is precisely why it is worth looking at, but a crash alone tells you nothing, you have to ask why. Here the moat, returns and recurring revenue are all intact, so it is not a Diageo; but the growth genuinely stalled and the recovery is a forecast rather than a fact, so it is not quite a RELX either. That lands it as a Watch on the edge of a Buy: quality clears the floor with room, the price is finally reasonable, and the only missing ingredient is proof that the destock has ended rather than management's word that it will. The disciplined move is not to chase the recovery on hope, nor to avoid a superb business out of memory of the fall, but to buy it a little cheaper or a little more proven. For your portfolio it has the added merit of being genuine ex-US diversification, which is a reason to keep it on a short leash rather than a long one.
