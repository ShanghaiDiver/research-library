01 · What the business owns

Zotefoams makes high-performance, closed-cell polymer foams, using nitrogen-based supercritical expansion and tightly controlled manufacturing processes. AZOTE covers polyolefin foams; ZOTEK covers technical polymers including demanding aerospace, medical and industrial applications; T-FIT covers insulation products. Footwear-related foams have made the company a remarkable supplier to a global sports brand ecosystem, but that customer relationship is a concentration risk, not an indefinite monopoly.

The moat consists of process science, repeatable foam structure and weight/performance properties, customer qualification, capital-intensive and hard-to-replicate plant know-how, and the cost of switching an approved material in a specialist application. The weaker part of the moat is any customer-specific footwear product where a powerful buyer can redesign or renegotiate. In technical transport and healthcare uses, qualification and certification can extend product life and customer stickiness.

02 · The growth algorithm, and the acquisitions accounting trap

The H1 2026 headline is strong: £95.2m revenue, +23%. Yet the acquired Overseas Konstellation Company (OKC) contributed £14.8m after its November 2025 acquisition. Excluding acquisitions, organic growth was only 4%, or 6% in constant currency. Diversification into transport, smart technologies and international markets cushioned expected footwear normalisation. This makes the strategic question sharp: can new specialist markets grow fast enough to justify manufacturing reinvestment while reducing dependence on one major footwear customer?

03 · Capital allocation: the factory expansion must produce returns

Management is building a more geographically balanced supply network: commissioning the Vietnam facility, integrating OKC, activating a Korean footwear innovation centre, deploying an approved-partner sales programme and restructuring its UK production footprint. These are material management and capital decisions. The company expects more than £230m revenue and £40m adjusted operating profit by FY2029. Those are targets, not contracted future cash flows. At present the business is mid-transition, with costs occurring before all benefits and with the usual risk that a new factory begins with low utilisation.

We should track incremental EBITDA and post-tax cash flow per pound invested into Vietnam, OKC and UK restructuring, plus return on capital employed including goodwill. A lower-margin acquired foam converter is not economically interchangeable with Zotefoams' highly differentiated material science operations. UK restructuring is intended to deliver roughly £4m of annual savings, but net savings after one-off costs and disruption should be measured, not assumed.

04 · Cash flow, working capital and financing

Net debt on the covenant definition increased to £39.1m from £21.1m a year before. It excludes lease liabilities, so it should not be treated as a full economic debt figure. A £90m revolving credit facility and £50.2m of liquidity headroom provide funding flexibility, but higher debt and new manufacturing requirements reduce room for mistakes in a small-cap cyclical shock.

The principal unresolved underwriting question is owner earnings after maintenance capex, growth capex, working-capital absorption and restructuring. A 34% rise in adjusted operating profit does not by itself establish a 34% rise in distributable cash. Profit and margin targets should be reconciled with cash generated from operations, cash paid for factories, lease commitments and acquired assets before changing our intrinsic valuation.

05 · Competitive advantages and where they can fail

The highest-value claim is process-led material performance combined with high customer qualification costs. The weakest point is dependence on major athletic footwear programmes: a customer can dominate order volume, negotiate pricing and switch specifications in ways a small supplier cannot control. Manufacturing disruption in an expensive specialist facility would be disproportionately painful. In addition, resin, energy, freight, tariffs and overseas currencies all influence reported profit. A balanced investment case recognises technical scarcity without treating customer relationships as irrevocable.

Competitors include global cellular polymer manufacturers and specialist materials suppliers; direct comparisons should reflect foam chemistry, end-use, required certification and internal process capability, not superficial price-to-sales multiples of commodity plastics businesses. The most useful disclosure from management would be customer and application concentration after OKC consolidation and incremental margin from non-footwear channels.

06 · Financial targets versus investor returns

Management's FY2029 ambition of >£230m revenue and >£40m adjusted operating profit implies at least a roughly 17.4% adjusted operating margin on those thresholds, although 'greater than' figures do not define the exact ratio. The company may deliver targets and still disappoint owners if high capital requirements or acquisition costs reduce cash returns. The relevant bridge is:

Operating earnings → cash tax and interest → maintenance investment → growth factory outlay → working capital → cash attributable to ordinary shares, measured across a complete capacity cycle.

A durable >10% owner-earnings growth rate could support a premium to industrial peers, but there is insufficient independently normalised per-share free cash flow here to defend a precise ten-year return forecast. We therefore do not manufacture one from a growth target alone.

08 · Management, incentives and governance

The board's strategic challenge is to move from reliance on footwear volume to a broader speciality-foam franchise without diluting quality of returns. Evaluate whether remuneration rewards cash ROIC and sustainable per-share wealth rather than acquisition-led revenue growth or adjusted operating-profit targets in isolation. Reported adjusted earnings exclusions, acquisition accounting and restructuring should be reconciled to total shareholder cash outlays across several years.

09 · The best bull and bear arguments

Bull: proprietary low-density performance foams, qualification barriers, differentiated manufacturing and multiple new applications deliver a long runway, improved margins and geographic diversification. OKC broadens routes to market and Vietnam provides local supply for Asian growth. Profits should become less sensitive to one footwear line over time.

Bear: the 23% headline revenue growth masks only 4% organic growth; Nike-linked or equivalent footwear exposure remains material; plants and acquisitions consume capital before uncertain returns arrive; net debt has almost doubled year on year. Even good technical products can be poor investments when customers capture most of the value or expansion capital earns less than expected.

10 · Currency and portfolio fit

Zotefoams is quoted in sterling but sells and operates internationally, with material inputs and sales in other currencies. It is more exposed to UK small-cap liquidity and manufacturing execution than the large compounders in the library. It could diversify a portfolio dominated by US technology and financial services, but its concentration risks are different, not necessarily lower. Once actual holdings are available, consider maximum acceptable position exposure to a single footwear customer, geographical production and a small-cap liquidity event.

11 · Catalysts and monitoring

Near term: October Vietnam trial-production progress, order levels in footwear versus non-footwear, OKC integration and UK cost restructuring; next FY2026 results in 2027. Medium term: customer and channel diversification, capacity utilisation, gross profit per plant, net debt including leases, capital expenditure, cash conversion and incremental return on capital including acquisition goodwill. A convincing owner-earnings record over two or three periods is more valuable than meeting a single annual adjusted-profit target.

12 · Verdict

Watch, rather than Buy by default. The proprietary process and application breadth are real advantages, and the business has improved operating margins. But nearly all headline H1 revenue growth beyond 4% organic expansion came from acquisition contribution and currency; we need to demonstrate that the investment programme produces more cash per share. At the present price the old house fair value provides no compelling cushion. An initial position could eventually be warranted on improving cash economics or a materially better price, but concentration and factory/project risks matter.
