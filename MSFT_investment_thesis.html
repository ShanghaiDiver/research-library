Q1

FY2027 Q1: 28 October results and thesis implications

What changed: the 28 October announcement for the quarter ended 30 September 2026 answered the monitoring event flagged by the 26 October report. Growth and enterprise distribution remain strong, with evidence of better infrastructure utilisation. Yet the capital burden is still rising, and reported accounting profit is not the same as cash left for owners.

The decisive new evidence

Demand is not the weak link today. Microsoft stated that Azure demand still exceeds capacity, Foundry revenue more than tripled, and engineering recovered previously underused CPU capacity. The question is how much of this greater utilisation will persist as new capacity enters service, model pricing changes and fast-depreciating hardware must be replaced.

Backlog quality: commercial bookings declined 12% against unusually large prior-year Azure agreements even as remaining performance obligation rose 73%. The RPO weighted-average duration was 2.3 years, with ~30% expected to convert into revenue within 12 months. It would be mistaken to treat the entire $677bn as near-term, evenly profitable demand; large counterparty commitments, especially AI-related, should be tracked separately where disclosed.

Guidance and capex: management projected Azure constant-currency growth of 49–50% in FY27 Q2, Agents and Infra revenue of $80.1–80.7bn, and Q2 total capex exceeding $50bn. It indicated calendar-2026 capex around $170bn, reflecting some third-party capacity delays and a stepped-up subsequent delivery schedule, and expected FY27 free cash flow to remain positive. Calendar-year capex is not a fiscal-year forecast and is not directly interchangeable with cash property-and-equipment additions.

Assessment: higher confidence in the durability of cloud demand and Microsoft's ability to monetise its installed estate; no corresponding increase in confidence about replacement-adjusted owner cash flows. At ~$18.6bn quarterly FCF against ~$36.4bn adjusted earnings, the earnings-to-owner-cash gap is material. This is not an argument for selling an appropriately sized holding; it is a reason not to upgrade the fair value or fresh-capital classification without a new valuation.

Primary evidence: Microsoft FY27 Q1 results, 28 October 2026; official earnings-call transcript; FY27 Q1 Form 10-Q.

01

The business and the moat

Microsoft is not simply a bet on Azure. It is an interconnected enterprise system: identity, Windows, Microsoft 365, security, developer tools, databases and cloud infrastructure. Those products lower the organisational cost of choosing more Microsoft and raise the cost of leaving. The switching-cost moat is real, although customer dependence also gives regulators and large enterprises reason to resist the bundle.

AI is an extension of this distribution advantage, not yet proof of superior incremental returns. Copilot can monetise an installed workforce and Azure can supply inference and training, but utilisation and unit economics still matter. The strongest reason to own Microsoft is recurring enterprise demand, not an assumption that every AI initiative succeeds.

Cash conversion tells a less exuberant story than earnings. Operating cash flow has roughly doubled since FY2022, but cash spending on property and equipment has risen nearly fivefold. FY2026 reported free cash flow is roughly $67bn, below its FY2024 level, despite record operating income. Microsoft still generates positive free cash flow, unlike Amazon on its recent trailing figures, but the difference in capital intensity is narrowing.

Earnings quality and asset life

FY2026 GAAP earnings include investment-related gains: adjusted diluted EPS was $17.28 versus GAAP $17.95. Longer-lived data-centre assets and finance leases complicate period-to-period comparability. Gross FCF is not owner earnings after replacement obligations.

04

The look ahead

Archived pre-results expectations, written 26 October: Street expectations were optimistic heading into the 28 October earnings release: FY2027 revenue estimates around $391bn and the following year around $469bn in one aggregated forecast. That is sentiment evidence, not the basis for our valuation. The subsequent FY27 Q1 release confirmed Azure +48%, but commercial bookings were −12% against a demanding prior year, and investment intensified. Key tests now are sustainable Azure monetisation, commercial bookings normalised for large contracts, data-centre utilisation, and the slope of cash capex.

05

Management and capital allocation

Satya Nadella has turned a powerful legacy distribution system into a cloud-first business, while retaining a disciplined enterprise subscription engine. The next test is more capital-intensive: roughly two-thirds of Q4 capital expenditure went to relatively short-lived equipment. Management has shown the ability to monetise platforms, yet capacity purchased at peak AI demand could earn less than historical ROIC if pricing falls or customers self-provision.

Shareholder distributions continued, with more than $43bn returned in FY2026. That is an important counterweight to the capex expansion, but neither dividends nor repurchases absolve management from earning an appropriate return on the next $100bn of infrastructure.

At $524.51 on 23 October, Microsoft traded on about 29× reported FY2026 EPS and approximately 30× earnings adjusted for the investment effect, before considering further growth. The distinction matters more than a comparison with an arbitrary broker target.

07

Long-term value and the compounding test

The August house note placed fair value near $540 with a base expected long-term return of roughly 13% a year, and identified an add band below $510. Those estimates remain our prior thesis, not facts refreshed by today’s share price. At $524.51 the company has entered the former hold region, rather than a new fear-driven entry point.

Important: these are retained August scenario values, not a newly recalibrated DCF. The midpoint has only ~3% headroom to the 23 October quote. We should not represent the August 13% long-term return as an assured return from the higher current entry price. Under a simple five-year EPS-growth test, 12% annual EPS growth and a 25× exit multiple on normalised $17.28 earnings imply about $761 in five years, roughly 7.8% annualised capital appreciation before dividends. At 8% growth and a 22× exit multiple, the same calculation yields about $559, close to 1.3% annualised. These are explicit sensitivities, not price targets.

08

Market expectations and analyst thinking

Analyst sentiment is strongly positive. An S&P Global aggregation covered 57 analysts with an average target of about $602, spanning $440 to $870 as at 23 October. That broad range demonstrates the uncertainty around AI economics even while the prevailing recommendation is bullish.

Targets were being raised during October, particularly around expectations of Azure demand. Our variant question is whether stronger demand ultimately translates into higher owner earnings after recurring infrastructure replacement. Street optimism is a useful measure of what could disappoint, not a reason to raise intrinsic value automatically.

09

The case both ways and variant perception

Bull: Microsoft has a rare combination of platform dependence, an incumbent sales channel and high-margin productivity products. It can turn AI into an additional subscription and cloud service without needing to win every model-level innovation race.

Bear: the next marginal dollar of compute is not the same as the prior dollar invested in enterprise software. Depreciation, power, networking, GPU obsolescence, third-party model dependency and competitive price pressure can reduce incremental returns.

Variant perception

The market is increasingly convinced that demand justifies capex. The less settled question is whether replacement-adjusted cash generation will keep pace. High growth can coexist with declining returns on incremental invested capital.

10

Portfolio fit and sterling lens

The original note highlighted a US-heavy portfolio and indirect ownership through index trackers. A direct Microsoft holding compounds this concentration, despite its global customers. Currency is USD at quotation and substantially USD-oriented in investment economics, although worldwide revenue creates considerable operational FX exposure.

Verdict: Watch for fresh capital, retain if already held and appropriately sized. This is a deliberate distinction from the August document’s Buy: the business is not worse, but the October reference price offers less valuation protection. Do not mechanically sell because a historical price target was reached. Add on weakness only if evidence supports capex returns.

Post-results decision, 9 November: continue to hold the existing, appropriately sized ISA position; classify as Watch for additional capital. The 28 October results strengthened the demand thesis but did not settle capex economics. The $510/$650 prior valuation-review levels must be re-underwritten before being used for a new transaction.

12

What would change the call and review record

  • Conviction rises: Azure and M365 AI monetisation improve margins while cash capex growth moderates; backlog converts into cash.
  • Conviction falls: AI capacity remains underutilised, cash conversion weakens structurally, or accounting-life choices obscure economic replacement needs.
  • Price action: a fall alone creates no buy; compare a fresh price against re-underwritten value.

Decision log: August valuation: base $540, add below $510; 26 October review: Watch at a dated $524.51 reference; 9 November after FY27 Q1: Watch retained, stronger Azure/AI utilisation evidence offset by accelerating short-lived infrastructure investment. No new intrinsic valuation or automatic transaction signal. The FY27 Q2 Azure outlook and FY27 cash-flow progression are the next tests.
