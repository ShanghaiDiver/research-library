01

The business

Microsoft is three great franchises fused into one: the Azure cloud, the Microsoft 365 productivity suite (now with Copilot), and the enterprise stack from Windows to GitHub to security. What makes it a compounder is the switching cost, once an organisation runs on Microsoft's cloud, identity and productivity tools, leaving is close to unthinkable, so revenue is recurring, pricing power is real, and the whole thing throws off enormous cash at a 26% return on invested capital.

02

What changed since we bought at $379

The thesis was that AI-capex fear had made a superb business cheap, and that the backlog would prove the spending was demand-led. It has. FY2026 revenue rose ~18% to $332bn and earnings 31% to $134bn ($18 EPS); Azure crossed $100bn of annual revenue; and the commercial remaining-performance-obligation backlog reached $678bn, up 84%, which is hard evidence the capex is being pre-sold rather than hoped for. The shares re-rated from ~$379 toward ~$491, most of the gap to our old ~$480 fair value now closed. Two honesty flags carried over from the review: part of prior EPS beats included one-off investment gains, and Microsoft extended its data-centre depreciation life from 15 to 25 years, which flatters future earnings by accounting rather than operations. The core, though, is genuinely strong.

03

The long-term return test

At ~25 times forward, the question is not whether Microsoft is cheap but what a decade-long owner earns. Earnings compound at roughly 15% (Azure and AI-led, plus buybacks), the dividend adds ~0.7%, so before any multiple change the owner earns ~15% a year. Hold the multiple and that is the return; a de-rating trims it. In dollars:

04

The case, both ways, and the variant

Bull. The best-positioned incumbent in enterprise AI, with the distribution (every large company already a customer), the cloud, the OpenAI relationship and the balance sheet to win, compounding at 15% with 26% returns and a backlog that de-risks the spending.

Bear. The capex is staggering (~$190bn a year and rising), the returns on the newest AI infrastructure are unproven, the depreciation-life change and a shift to usage-based AI pricing make future earnings harder to forecast, and 25 times leaves little room if the payoff disappoints.

Variant perception

The market's oscillation on Microsoft is really an oscillation on one question: when does a $190bn capex number stop looking like vertigo and start looking like foresight. The backlog is the strongest available answer, $678bn up 84% says customers are committing years of spending in advance, which is exactly what you would not see if this were a bubble. The variant view we held, and still hold, is that Microsoft is the safest way to own the AI build-out precisely because its spending is underwritten by contracted demand rather than speculative capacity. The bear's fair point is that the marginal AI dollar's return is still unproven and the accounting has grown more flattering. So the discipline now differs from a year ago: at $379 the move was to buy aggressively into fear; at $491 it is to hold a compounder that is working and add on the dips, not to chase it higher.

05

Currency and fit

Dollar earnings in a dollar quote, so for a sterling holder Microsoft is pure US exposure, and it deepens a book already ~70% American. You own it inside the S&P and NASDAQ trackers too, so a direct position is a deliberate overweight of the single best megacap rather than diversification. Worth holding for the quality; worth sizing with the concentration in mind.

06

What would change the call

  • Downgrade if Azure growth decelerates below ~30%, if AI capex keeps climbing with no visible revenue payoff, or if the multiple runs toward ~30× without an acceleration to justify it.
  • Conviction rises if Copilot and Azure-AI monetisation show up as an acceleration in commercial growth, converting the capex into compounding.
07

Verdict

An elite compounder whose thesis has played out and is still intact. Track A. Fair value ~$540 against ~$491, base return ~13% a year. Action: Buy / hold your winner, add on weakness, size for the US concentration.

Microsoft did exactly what v2 was built to do: it was bought when AI-capex fear made a superb business cheap, and it has re-rated as the numbers, Azure past $100bn, an 84% backlog surge, 31% earnings growth, answered that fear. The stock is no longer the bargain it was at $379, but the long-term return test still clears the hurdle comfortably, so the compounder stays a Buy. The change is in temperament, not verdict: this is now a core holding to run and add to on dips, guided by the rule that you never trim a compounder simply for rising. The two things to keep honest about are the capex payoff, still unproven at the margin, and the concentration, since a direct MSFT position stacks on top of the large slug you already own through the index trackers.
