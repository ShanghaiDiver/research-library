01

The business

Adyen is a single, unified payments platform built from one codebase, which processes card and digital payments for large global merchants, online and in-store, across borders, on one integration. That architectural purity, most rivals are stitched together from acquisitions, gives it better authorisation rates, richer data and lower cost, which is why it wins and keeps enterprise customers. The economics are exceptional: capital-light, ~50% EBITDA margins, and a land-and-expand model where its share of a merchant's volume climbs from under 20% in the early years to over 40% by year twelve, a long, visible runway of growth from customers it already has.

02

Why the verdict changes

The old note made Adyen a Watch for one reason only: it pays no dividend, which under the original income-first mandate was disqualifying. v2 removes that gate, income is now a portfolio-level preference and buybacks count as shareholder return, so a superb no-yield compounder is welcome on its merits. On those merits Adyen is emphatic. H1 2026: net revenue €1.3bn, up 19% (21% constant-currency); processed volume €804bn, up 24%; EBITDA €642m at a 49-50% margin; free-cash-flow conversion 86%. Full-year guidance is 21-23% revenue growth, and the platform is widening decisively beyond payments, its first-ever acquisitions (Talon.One in loyalty, Orb in billing) plus Intelligent Money Movement and an agentic-commerce product, while winning names like OpenAI and Toast. It is becoming the financial operating system for modern commerce.

03

The long-term return test

It has re-rated hard since the last note (from the mid-€800s), so the easy value is gone and it trades at ~28 times forward, a premium. But v2 pays a full multiple for a genuine compounder, because the compounding does the work. Net revenue grows ~20%+, margins expand toward 55%, so earnings can compound faster than revenue; there is no yield, but buybacks add to per-share growth. In euros:

04

The case, both ways, and the variant

Bull. A rare architectural moat, 20%+ growth with a decade-long land-and-expand runway, 50%+ margins, expanding from payments into a whole financial platform, at a premium that a business compounding this fast can outgrow.

Bear. Payments is competitive (Stripe, banks, PayPal), take-rates drift down over time, the newest acquisitions are unproven and a departure from a 20-year organic record, capex is stepping up (to ~7% of revenue), and 28 times leaves no room for a growth stumble.

Variant perception

The market oscillates between pricing Adyen as a hyper-growth platform and as a payments processor facing inevitable take-rate compression; the multiple has swung violently on which view dominates. The variant view is that the single-platform architecture and the land-and-expand economics make Adyen structurally different from a commodity processor, its growth comes mainly from doing more for merchants it already has, not from winning price wars, and the move into loyalty, billing and money movement deepens that lock-in. The bear's fair point is that the first-ever acquisitions and the capex step-up introduce genuine execution risk into a story that was previously pristine, and the multiple prices success. So the verdict is a Buy rather than a table-pound: the quality and the long-term return justify it, but the premium means it should be built as a position over time and on weakness, not chased.

05

Currency and fit, for a sterling holder

The best fit of any Buy in the book. Adyen is euro-quoted and European-headquartered, though its merchant base and processed volume are global, so it brings genuine ex-US, ex-UK exposure of exactly the kind the look-through said the portfolio lacks, and it does so through a single high-quality name rather than a passive regional fund. Where RELX, Microsoft and Alphabet are all Buys that deepen the US tilt, Adyen is the Buy that helps correct it. The euro translation adds some year-to-year noise for a sterling owner, but as a diversifier it does a job none of the others do.

06

What would change the call

  • Downgrade if net-revenue growth slows below ~15% on a sustained basis, if take-rates compress faster than volume growth compensates, or if the acquisitions dilute margins or returns rather than extending the platform.
  • Conviction rises if the platform products (loyalty, billing, money movement) show up as an acceleration in share-of-wallet and net revenue, proving the expansion beyond payments is real.
07

Verdict

An elite growth compounder, freed by v2 from the one objection that parked it, and the rare Buy that also diversifies. Track A. Fair value ~€1,200 against ~€1,050, base return ~15%. Action: Buy, built on weakness given the premium multiple.

Adyen is the clearest illustration of why v2 needed writing. Under the old income-first rules it was a superb business we could not own, disqualified for paying no dividend; under v2, with buybacks counted and income a portfolio-level matter, it is judged on what it is, a capital-light, 20%-growth, 50%-margin platform with a decade of visible expansion, and it is a Buy. It has re-rated, so this is not the bargain of a year ago, and the premium multiple and the first-ever acquisitions mean it should be accumulated rather than chased. Its particular value to this portfolio is that it is the one high-conviction Buy that adds European exposure rather than more America, which is a reason to prefer it at the margin when new money is being put to work.
