# Investment discovery inbox

**Started:** 27 October 2026  
**Stage:** Discovery only — not a recommendation, watchlist verdict or live price screen.

This inbox captures unfamiliar or under-researched opportunities **before** committing to a full investment thesis. It complements rather than changes [the researched company register](../companies.json). It must not silently turn source opinions into investment judgements.

## Purpose

Broaden the opportunity set across sectors, countries, company sizes and investing styles; explore what we do not already know. The emphasis is on *how an investment might generate a satisfactory return*, not merely finding companies conventionally labelled 'exceptional'.

## Four provisional routes to an attractive investment

1. **Durable compounding:** sustainable customer advantage, reinvestment runway and attractive per-share cash generation.
2. **Operational improvement:** a credible change in management, business mix, productivity or capital allocation.
3. **Mispriced cash generation or assets:** owner distributions, sum-of-parts or overlooked cash flow at a suitable price.
4. **Cyclical or special situation:** market pessimism, industry cycles or a discrete event create prospective mispricing.

A candidate can fit more than one path, and a specialist business can deteriorate despite good historical metrics. Assess changes in *customer proposition and management understanding*—including the loss of supporting product categories or reasons to visit—as well as the accounts.

## Working process

**Inbox → Explore → Research → Watch / Reject / Investable review**

- **Inbox:** A lead. Record the source and date, one-line business, *hypothetical* reason to be interested and principal question. No unsourced 'Buy' labels.
- **Explore:** Brief primary-source review: how money is made, customer reason to choose the firm, competitive/capital position, track record, ownership and likely takeover vulnerability, and why the market may be wrong. Seek disconfirming evidence.
- **Research:** Earns an appropriately detailed library report. Still not an instruction to trade.
- **Watch / Reject:** Document what would change the decision, including improving versus eroding business quality, or opportunity-cost reasons. Rejected candidates are retained as learning, not erased.
- **Investable review:** Only for a real decision and plausible price opportunity: refresh evidence, valuation, prospective shareholder returns, risk and portfolio fit. No forced full DCF at Inbox stage.

Preserve the difference between **business quality**, **business-quality trajectory**, **prospective investment returns**, **shareholder participation/takeover risks** and **portfolio role**. No fixed country allocation or requirement that every investment be an enduring compounder. The listed source is a discovery prompt, not an endorsement.

## Sources and evidence

The seed ideas in [candidates.csv](candidates.csv) come from the user's October 2026 mentions of *Times Tempus* and Ian Cowie's disclosed holdings list. Cowie portfolio entries are **user-supplied leads, not verified current positions**. The Tempus headlines and any article views must be checked against the actual text when available. **No paywalled text has been copied.** The 'why interesting' fields are initial hypotheses, not assertions of proven moats or financial performance. Tickers are initial identifiers requiring verification before use.

Further candidate sources: annual reports, company and competitor disclosures, quantitative screens, long-term investors' letters, niche industry journals, customer-product ecosystems, and selected investment trusts. Look for genuinely *new names and mechanisms*, rather than repeatedly rediscovering large US technology businesses held indirectly in the portfolio.

## Discipline and upkeep

- One row per candidate in CSV; keep dates and attribution. Expand only after evidence warrants it.
- Prefer a short set of informative leads over an indiscriminate weekly dump.
- Do not conflate corporate acquisitions with investment success: forced sales can interrupt compounding or crystallise unattractive returns.
- Evaluate past decisions using information available *then*, not hindsight alone.
- Keep the user's portfolio and cash balances **out of the public repository**; company research may be public, account-level financial information should not be added without consent.
- Future monitoring may propose changes through draft PRs only. **Never automatically invest, publish or merge.**
