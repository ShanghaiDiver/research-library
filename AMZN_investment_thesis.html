Amazon.com Inc

NASDAQ: AMZN · $248.00 close on 23 Oct 2026Verdict: Watch, provisional

Demand has accelerated. The cash harvest has not. AWS grew 37% in the June quarter, advertising 26%, and operating profit rose sharply. Yet $169bn of trailing cash capital expenditure has pushed reported free cash flow below zero. The question for a long-term owner is no longer whether Amazon has attractive franchises. It is what the latest marginal investment dollar earns, and whether those returns can compound for shareholders.

These are not trading signals. A credible price band requires a defensible owner-earnings model and an explicit opportunity-cost test.

01

The business and its moat

Amazon combines a high-volume retail and fulfilment network, third-party seller services, subscriptions, advertising and AWS. Retail creates traffic and purchase-intent data; the marketplace and fulfilment network make that traffic valuable to sellers; advertising monetises discovery; Prime rewards frequency. AWS is a distinct infrastructure and software platform with substantial switching costs, but an increasingly capital-intensive one.

The attraction for a patient owner is the possibility that cloud and advertising cash generation continue to strengthen while logistics scale improves retail economics. The danger is mistaking growing operating profit for incremental shareholder value when each new wave of infrastructure absorbs still more capital.

02

What changed since the June note

The June thesis described AWS growing around 20–24%. Amazon's Q2 2026 release instead reported AWS sales growth of 37%, the fastest in 18 quarters, alongside 26% advertising growth and operating income of $27.5bn. The evidence of demand is substantially better than the earlier note suggested.

But trailing operating cash flow of $161.4bn was exceeded by $169.0bn of cash capital expenditure, leaving free cash flow of −$7.6bn. The original thesis's concern about conversion of reinvestment into cash therefore remains live, even as demand improves. Source: Amazon Q2 2026 release.

Accounting honesty. Q2 2026 net income includes $53.4bn of non-operating pre-tax other income, primarily Anthropic investment remeasurement. Neither the one-off gain nor trailing P/E built on it is an adequate proxy for sustainable owner earnings.

Stock compensation, leases and the full cost of replacing AI infrastructure also matter. There is no published maintenance-versus-growth-capex split sufficient to permit a confident owner-earnings adjustment. Pretending otherwise would make the model look precise while hiding its largest uncertainty.

04

The long-term compounding test

The relevant test is not a 12-month target. It is whether Amazon can reinvest at returns exceeding its cost of capital, sustain those returns for years and convert the resulting asset base into distributable cash. Advertising and marketplace economics may be capital-light relative to revenue; AI data centres are not. The marginal economics of the latter dominate the central valuation question today.

Five-year operating scenarios should therefore forecast cash generated by the business, capital committed to sustaining and expanding it, the useful life of that capital, and the cash available to owners. The correct comparison is between the incremental return earned and the return that capital could have earned elsewhere.

06

The case, both ways

Bull at its strongest. AWS growth is accelerating materially, custom silicon and AI demand may fill incremental capacity, advertising compounds off a differentiated commerce ecosystem, and management has repeatedly demonstrated the ability to build new profit pools by investing ahead of demand.

Bear at its strongest. Cloud infrastructure may need repeated replacement before the full economic return is realised. AI pricing could compress, customer concentration could rise, or power and depreciation costs could absorb gains. Growing revenue and reported operating earnings do not establish that incremental capital earns its keep.

Variant perception. A bullish variant is not simply that demand is strong, the quarterly numbers show that. It is that the present capex cycle will produce sustained, attractive incremental returns after replacement and maintenance costs. The bearish variant is that infrastructure becomes a rapidly depreciating competitive necessity rather than an exceptional source of owner earnings.
07

Ownership, portfolio fit and currency

For a sterling investor, Amazon introduces direct dollar equity exposure and additional US megacap concentration, including overlap with broad market trackers. A holding should be judged not merely against its own fair value, but against alternative uses of scarce portfolio capital.

Buy and hold is active ownership. Quarterly results should update the investment record: original expectation, observed outcome, changed assumption, and implication for future owner earnings. A holding does not become a sale merely because its quoted price rises.

08

Falsification and next review

  • Conviction rises if AWS and advertising continue producing profitable growth while operating cash flow increasingly outruns the cost of added capacity.
  • Conviction falls if capital spending continues accelerating without corresponding incremental cash returns, or if dilution and financing commitments consume the gains.
  • Next event: Amazon Q3 2026 results, 29 October 2026. This document stops at information available on 26 October and must not imply those results are known.
09

Verdict

Watch, pending valuation completion. The business case has improved; the owner-earnings case is not yet proved.

The June report's emphasis on weak AWS relative growth is stale after Q2. That change improves the operational thesis materially. Yet the central hurdle, long-term returns on a very large and rising capital base, cannot be resolved from reported free cash flow alone. The right action is to preserve the evidence, complete a genuinely independent capital-return valuation and reassess after Q3, not to manufacture a precise price target from uncertain capex classifications.
