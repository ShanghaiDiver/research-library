Diageo

LSE: DGE · ~1,750p · 25 Aug 2026 Verdict: Watch

One of the great consumer franchises in the world, Johnnie Walker, Guinness, Don Julio, Tanqueray, Smirnoff, down more than half from its peak and hated. Exactly the kind of quality-on-sale the framework wants to examine. But this is the mirror of RELX: where RELX was marked down on a fear, Diageo is marked down on real trouble, US tequila collapsing, China weak, growth stalled, leverage over the line and the dividend just cut. The moat is real; the numbers do not yet clear the bar. That makes it a Watch, not a bargain.

v2 basis: a quality franchise fails the compounder gates here (growth under 5%, leverage over 3×, dividend cut), so it sits on the value track and needs a real margin of safety. At ~1,750p there isn't one. A cheaper price, or clear stabilisation, turns it into a Buy.

01

The business, and why it screens

Diageo is the world's leading premium spirits company, an irreplaceable portfolio of brands with real pricing power, global distribution and genuine emerging-market reach (India, Africa, Latin America), which is part of why the screen flagged it as a way to buy international exposure in a sterling wrapper. On moat and cash generation it is a quality business. But it appears here not as a compounder, it fails that test right now, but as a fallen quality name to interrogate: is the collapse in the shares a cyclical trough in a great franchise, or the start of a structural decline?

02

The trouble is real

This is not a sentiment problem. Organic net sales grew just 1.7% in FY2025 and fell ~2% in FY2026; operating profit is barely moving. The damage is concentrated but severe: North America organic sales fell 8.4% with tequila down ~21% as Casamigos and Don Julio deflated a post-pandemic bubble, and Asia-Pacific fell ~6% on Chinese weakness. Against that, India grew ~7% and Europe, Latin America and Africa held up, with Guinness a genuine bright spot. Net debt sits at $21.9bn, a leverage ratio of 3.1-3.4 times that breaches the framework's gate, and in early 2026 the Board cut the dividend to rebuild the balance sheet, roughly halving a payout that had grown for decades.

Management's answer is a $625m cost programme, $2.3bn of disposals (East African Breweries to Asahi) to deleverage, and a strategy reset guiding low-single-digit sales and mid-single-digit profit growth over three years. Credible, but a three-year plan to grow mid-single-digits is not a compounder, it is a recovery.

03

Value: fallen, not yet cheap enough

At ~1,750p Diageo trades on roughly 15 times forward earnings, against a 52-week range of 1,351-2,142p and a peak above 4,000p. That is a long way down, but "down a lot" is not the same as "cheap enough". On the value track we want a real discount to a conservative fair value, and the leverage and absent growth mean that fair value is not far above the price. In pence:

04

The case, both ways

Bull. An irreplaceable brand portfolio with pricing power, at ~15 times and half off, in a rare cyclical trough driven by a tequila destock and a temporary China air-pocket. Deleveraging is underway, costs are coming out, India and Africa grow, and premium spirits have recovered from every prior downturn. Buy great brands when nobody wants them.

Bear. The troubles may be structural, not cyclical: GLP-1 weight-loss drugs and a genuine generational shift toward drinking less could cap spirits demand for years, tequila was a bubble, China may not bounce, and a balance sheet at 3.4 times leaves little room if profit keeps sliding, which is why the dividend had to be cut. Cheap franchises can stay cheap while they de-rate.

Variant perception

The market is pricing the start of a structural decline. The bull's variant view is that this is a classic quality-in-a-trough, the brands are intact, the destock is finite, and patient buyers are handed a great business at a fair price. The trouble with siding with the bull today is that, unlike RELX, the pessimism here is grounded in things that are actually happening, not merely feared: sales are falling, leverage is over the line, and the dividend has been cut. The GLP-1 and moderation questions are real and unresolved, not obviously overblown. So the honest verdict is not that the bears are right, it is that quality alone does not clear the bar when the balance sheet is stretched, growth is absent and the structural question is genuinely open. This is exactly the discipline that stopped us chasing Apple: a wonderful business is not automatically a buy. Watch, and demand either a cheaper price or hard evidence the trough has passed.

05

What would change the call

  • Upgrade to Buy on a lower price (toward ~1,450p, a real margin of safety), or on evidence the trough has passed: US and tequila stabilising, leverage back below 3×, organic growth returning to mid-single-digits, and the rebased dividend comfortably covered.
  • Downgrade towards Avoid if GLP-1 or moderation show up as sustained volume decline across markets, if leverage rises rather than falls, or if the recovery plan slips.
06

Verdict

A great franchise in genuine trouble, fallen a long way but not yet cheap enough, with the balance sheet over the line and the structural question open. Track B. Fair value ~1,700p against ~1,750p. Action: Watch, buy below ~1,450p or on clear stabilisation.

Diageo is the instructive counterpart to RELX. Both are quality names the market has marked down hard; the difference is why. RELX fell on a fear the numbers contradict, so it is a Buy. Diageo fell on troubles the numbers confirm, stalled growth, a stretched balance sheet, a cut dividend, an unresolved question about whether spirits demand is structurally impaired, so it is a Watch, however good the brands. The framework's job here is not to fall in love with Johnnie Walker but to insist on being paid for the risk, and at ~1,750p it isn't. As a bonus, it would help the international gap if it were a Buy, its earnings are genuinely global, but wanting the diversification is not a reason to relax the price discipline. Keep it on a short leash: a slide toward the low, or a clear turn in the US and the leverage, and this becomes interesting quickly.
